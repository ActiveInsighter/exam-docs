<ExamArticle title="The value of paying attention" source="原创模拟文章 · 非历年真题">

<p>On an ordinary Tuesday, a group of volunteers walked through a small city park. Some listened to a podcast, while others were asked to put away their phones and notice the sounds, colours, and shapes around them. <ExamKeySentence id="p1s3">At the end of the walk, the second group felt calmer and remembered more of what they had seen.</ExamKeySentence> The difference surprised several volunteers, who had expected a familiar park to offer little worth noticing.</p>

<p>The following week, the volunteers returned to the park. They visited the same place, but this time they exchanged activities: those who had listened to a podcast now walked without headphones, and the others did the opposite. Once again, the people who observed their surroundings recalled more details. <ExamKeySentence id="p2s4">Because both groups experienced both activities in the same setting, the comparison helped the researchers separate the effect of attention from the appeal of a particular location.</ExamKeySentence></p>

<p><ExamKeySentence id="p3s1">The researchers concluded that it was the way people directed their attention, rather than simply being outdoors, that made the difference.</ExamKeySentence> <ExamKeySentence id="p3s2">A tree they had passed a hundred times became interesting again; a bird call that had once disappeared into background noise became distinct.</ExamKeySentence> <ExamKeySentence id="p3s3">This does not mean that podcasts are harmful, or that every walk should become a formal exercise.</ExamKeySentence> <ExamKeySentence id="p3s4">It suggests, instead, that constantly filling our attention can leave little room for experiences already within reach.</ExamKeySentence></p>

<p>We often imagine that a memorable experience requires a distant journey or an expensive purchase. <ExamKeySentence id="p4s2">Yet the park experiment points to a more modest possibility: we can find something new in familiar surroundings by looking at them with fresh attention.</ExamKeySentence> The ordinary need not remain invisible just because it is ordinary. Before searching for a different world, we might begin by making a little more space to notice the one we already inhabit.</p>

</ExamArticle>

<ExamQuestion>

<p lang="en">1. Why did the volunteers exchange activities during their second visit?</p>

<ExamChoices>
<ExamOption>A. To discover which part of the park was most attractive.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>B. To distinguish the effect of attention from that of the setting.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>C. To test whether a new podcast could improve their memory.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>D. To encourage them to visit unfamiliar places.</ExamOption>
</ExamChoices>

<ExamSolution evidence={['p2s4']}>
<ExamAnswer>B</ExamAnswer>
<ExamExplanation>

定位第二段末句。separate the effect of attention from… 对应选项中的 distinguish…from…。交换活动让两组都体验两种方式，同时控制地点，是为了判断差异是否来自注意力。

- A：原文保持地点不变，没有比较公园的不同区域。
- C：实验并未更换或评估播客内容。
- D：same setting 表明两次都在同一个熟悉的地点。

</ExamExplanation>
</ExamSolution>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion>

<p lang="en">2. What can we infer from the results of the two walks?</p>

<ExamChoices>
<ExamOption>A. A change of scenery is necessary for a memorable experience.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>B. Listening to a podcast always makes people anxious.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>C. Paying attention to nearby details can enrich an ordinary experience.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>D. People remember places better when they walk alone.</ExamOption>
</ExamChoices>

<ExamSolution evidence={['p1s3', 'p3s1']}>
<ExamAnswer>C</ExamAnswer>
<ExamExplanation>

第一段给出初次结果，第三段概括原因：决定差异的是注意力的分配方式。由此可推知，留意身边细节可以改善平常的体验，而不需要改变地点。注意 always、necessary 等绝对表述。

- A：地点未变，体验却发生了变化；necessary 扩大了结论。
- B：原文没有证明焦虑，也明确反对将播客视为有害。
- D：独自散步不是实验比较的变量。

</ExamExplanation>
</ExamSolution>

</ExamQuestion>

<p lang="en">3. The word “distinct” in Paragraph 3 is closest in meaning to _____.</p>

<ExamChoices>
<ExamOption>A. clearly noticeable</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>B. completely unfamiliar</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>C. unusually unpleasant</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>D. physically distant</ExamOption>
</ExamChoices>

<ExamSolution evidence={['p3s2']}>
<ExamAnswer>A</ExamAnswer>
<ExamExplanation>

根据同一句的对照猜词：过去鸟鸣 disappeared into background noise，如今 became distinct。变化在于从被忽略到可辨认，因此 distinct 表示「清楚可察觉的」。

- B：声音并不是新的，只是过去没有被留意。
- C：上下文没有负面的情绪评价。
- D：这里讨论感知的清晰程度，而非距离。

</ExamExplanation>
</ExamSolution>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion>

<p lang="en">4. Which statement best describes the author’s view of podcasts?</p>

<ExamChoices>
<ExamOption>A. They should be avoided whenever people go outdoors.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>B. They are the main cause of poor memory.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>C. They are more valuable than direct observation.</ExamOption>
<ExamOption>D. They are not necessarily harmful, but constant distraction has a cost.</ExamOption>
</ExamChoices>

<ExamSolution evidence={['p3s3', 'p3s4']}>
<ExamAnswer>D</ExamAnswer>
<ExamExplanation>

作者先用 This does not mean… 限定结论，随后用 instead 表明真正关注的问题：持续占满注意力可能压缩感受周围世界的空间。D 同时保留了这个让步与提醒。

- A：作者未要求户外活动时禁止播客。
- B：原文没有将记忆问题归因于播客。
- C：原文并未作出这种价值排序。

</ExamExplanation>
</ExamSolution>

</ExamQuestion>
